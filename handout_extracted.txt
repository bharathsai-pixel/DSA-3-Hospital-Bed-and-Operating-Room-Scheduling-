Syllabus : Module-1 (T extHack as a System — The Advanced-Algorithm Question Bank): •
A working text-analytics engine on day one — students issue queries against an
Indian-language Wikipedia corpus and observe results before any internals. • The
query classes TextHack answers: pattern search, fuzzy matching with edit
distance, document similarity , citation-flow analysis, project-scheduling
demonstrations, lar ge-prime primality testing. • How each query class maps to an
advanced-algorithm family: pattern search → string algorithms; fuzzy matching
→ DP; similarity → suf fix structures; flow → max-flow; scheduling → NP-hard
with approximation; primality → randomised algorithms. • Why the advanced
canon exists: linear and tree/graph algorithms from DSA-1/DSA-2 cannot solve
sub-quadratic substring search, optimal alignment, or capacity-constrained
assignment. • A reading of the algorithms textbook canon: CLRS Part VII,
Kleinber g-Tardos Chapters 6-13, Erickson Chapters 4-12 — the territory we
cover . • Continuation of the engine constraint from DSA-1/DSA-2: java.util.*
forbidden inside the engine; students hand-build every algorithm. • Reading
TextHack's public API end-to-end before any implementation work. • A first
design pass: which algorithm answers each query and what is its asymptotic cost?
Module-2 (String Algorithms): • The string-matching problem: pattern P  inside
text T; the naïve O(nm) algorithm and why we need better . • Knuth-Morris-Pratt
(KMP): the failure function, why the algorithm is linear , the pre-processing
intuition. • Z-function: the modern alternative to KMP; computing all Z-values in
linear time. • Rabin-Karp with polynomial rolling hash: the average-case linear
running time, the modular arithmetic discipline, double-hashing for collision
avoidance. • Multi-pattern matching with Aho-Corasick at intuition level: the trie-
with-failure-links construction. • Suf fix arrays: definition, the SA-IS construction
in O(n) at conceptual level, practical O(n log² n) implementation. • Longest
Common Prefix (LCP) array: the Kasai algorithm in linear time given a suf fix
array . • Suf fix automata and suf fix trees at preview level — what they enable that7/20/26, 11:23 AM about:blank

NP-hard structures. • Approximation algorithms: ratio guarantees, the worked
example of vertex-cover 2-approximation via maximal matching. • PT AS,
FPTAS, APX at intuition level; problems where (1+ε) approximations exist and
problems where they don't. • Parameterised complexity at preview level: FPT ,
kernelisation, problems where the exponential is in a small parameter , not the
input. Module-6 (Randomised and Parallel Algorithms): • Las Vegas vs Monte
Carlo: the dif ference between always-correct and correct-with-probability . •
Randomised quicksort revisited from DSA-1 with rigorous expected-time
analysis. • Miller -Rabin primality test: the witness construction, the Carmichael-

Randomised hashing: universal hash families, perfect hashing, why hashing is
fundamentally probabilistic. • Reservoir sampling for streaming data: the elegant

embarrassingly-parallel category . (GPU specifics belong to DLAC; distributed

Course Rationale :Data Structures and Algorithms - 3 closes the algorithm-design canon. After
DSA-1 (linear DS) and DSA-2 (trees, graphs, intro DP), this course owns the
advanced territory: string algorithms, deep dynamic programming, network
flows, NP-completeness with reductions and approximation, and the
randomised/parallel-algorithm foundations. The PBL  track is OUTSIDE-IN:
students see a working text-analytics engine on day one — pattern search, fuzzy
matching, document similarity , citation-flow analysis, scheduling — and across
twelve weeks they implement every algorithm beneath it. java.util.* remains
forbidden inside the engine. Strict no-overlap: distributed-system theory belongs
to SDSR (T06); GPU parallelism belongs to DLAC (T07). Pre-requisite: DSA-2.
Enables: every advanced-algorithm and AI course downstream. Placement
corridor: ₹20-40 LP A roles at product companies (Google, Microsoft, Amazon,
Meta) and quantitative firms (T ower Research, Optiver , Citadel) where advanced-
algorithm interview rounds (string DP , flow , NP-hard problem reductions) are
decisive at the senior -fresher level.
Course Objectives :Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to: 1.
Understand advanced algorithmic paradigms and their applicability to complex
computational problems arising in text processing, optimization, and lar ge-scale
data analysis. 2. Apply ef ficient string processing techniques, advanced dynamic
programming methods, and network flow algorithms to solve real-world
computational challenges. 3. Analyze the computational complexity of problems
using complexity classes, polynomial-time reductions, and NP-completeness
concepts. 4. Evaluate approximation techniques and parameterized approaches
for obtaining practical solutions to computationally intractable problems. 5.
Apply randomized and parallel algorithmic techniques to improve computational
efficiency , scalability , and performance in modern computing environments. 6.
Develop algorithmic solutions by integrating multiple advanced paradigms and
assessing their correctness, ef ficiency , and suitability for diverse application
domains.

PLAGIARISM POLICY :
Plagiarism is treated as a serious academic of fense, and the university enforces a strict zero-tolerance policy to
uphold academic integrity . For a first of fense, minor cases may result in a warning and reduced marks, while major
cases may lead to a zero grade and mandatory academic integrity training. A second of fense will result in a zero
grade, formal reprimand, and academic probation, whereas a third of fense may lead to suspension and a permanent
record of misconduct. Repeated violations can result in expulsion, and in group work, all members are held
accountable unless responsibility is clearly established.
